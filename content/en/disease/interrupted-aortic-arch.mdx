An interrupted aortic arch is a rare congenital heart defect in which a segment of the <Structure id="aorta">aorta</Structure> between its ascending and descending parts is missing or has no lumen.<Cite n={1} /> Blood reaches the descending aorta, and so the lower half of the body, only through the open ductus arteriosus.<Cite n={1} />

It is a critical defect of the newborn period: without treatment the outlook is extremely poor.<Cite n={1} /> The Russian Ministry of Health guideline on which this article is based is written for children.<Cite n={1} />

## Why it happens

<Patient>

The heart forms in the first trimester of pregnancy, and problems with its development at this time are especially dangerous.<Cite n={1} /> The exact cause of the defect is unknown. Known risk factors for congenital heart defects include long-term illnesses in the mother, such as diabetes.<Cite n={1} /> An interrupted aortic arch often occurs together with a genetic change, the 22q11.2 microdeletion (DiGeorge syndrome): according to the Russian Ministry of Health paediatric guideline, in more than 80% of cases.<Cite n={1} />

There are almost always other features of the heart as well. According to the Russian Ministry of Health paediatric guideline, an open ductus arteriosus and a [ventricular septal defect](../ventricular-septal-defect/) are present in at least 90% of cases.<Cite n={1} />

While the duct stays open, problems with the circulation may be relatively mild in the first days of life.<Cite n={1} /> But the duct can close at any moment, and then the lower half of the body is left without blood.<Cite n={1} /> That is why, with this defect, an open duct is vital for the baby until the operation.<Cite n={1} /> The [patent ductus arteriosus](../patent-ductus-arteriosus/) as a defect in its own right has a separate article.

At the same time, more and more blood goes to the lungs through the septal defect, and [heart failure](../heart-failure/) develops.<Cite n={1} /> Without treatment the baby's condition becomes critical: shock develops, and bleeding inside the skull and kidney failure may occur.<Cite n={1} />

</Patient>

<Student>

According to the Russian Ministry of Health paediatric guideline, an interrupted aortic arch is a loss of continuity of the vessel lumen or a complete absence of a segment between the ascending and descending aorta; flow to the descending aorta is maintained through a patent ductus arteriosus (PDA).<Cite n={1} /> The defect belongs to the congenital anomalies of the aortic arch and its branches.<Cite n={1} />

The Celoria–Patton classification (as modified by Oppenheimer-Dekker) is used by the Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) and is part of the international nomenclature of congenital heart defects: type A, interruption distal to the left subclavian artery; type B, between the subclavian and carotid arteries; type C, between the carotid arteries; each type has subtypes with a retro-oesophageal or isolated subclavian artery.<Cite n={1} /> According to the Russian Ministry of Health guideline, type A accounts for 30–41% of cases, type B for 55–69% and type C for 4–17%.<Cite n={1} />

The haemodynamics are determined by duct-dependent systemic blood flow and an intracardiac left-to-right shunt that causes volume overload.<Cite n={1} /> As pulmonary vascular resistance falls naturally, the shunt volume grows, and organ hypoperfusion, heart failure and lactic acidosis increase; they worsen when the PDA closes.<Cite n={1} />

According to the Russian Ministry of Health, associated defects are present in up to 98%: PDA and ventricular septal defect (VSD) in at least 90%, bicuspid aortic valve in up to 60%, and less often [truncus arteriosus](../truncus-arteriosus/), single ventricle, left ventricular outflow tract obstruction and transposition of the great arteries.<Cite n={1} /> In type B the VSD is very often an outlet defect, and truncus arteriosus and double outlet right ventricle are regarded as typical associated defects.<Cite n={1} />

According to the Russian Ministry of Health, interrupted aortic arch accounts for no more than 0.5% of all congenital heart defects and 1.5% of critical ones.<Cite n={1} /> For the natural history, the guideline cites autopsy data: in 75% of cases the deceased were no older than 1 month, and the median age at death was 4–10 days.<Cite n={1} />

The same guideline describes double aortic arch, a different defect: both arches persist and form a ring around the trachea and oesophagus. It does not disturb the circulation itself; the symptoms (inspiratory stridor, a barking cough, feeding difficulties) are caused by compression, and this defect does not depend on the PDA.<Cite n={1} />

</Student>

## Symptoms

<Patient>

This defect never runs its course without symptoms.<Cite n={1} /> From the first days of life the baby often develops breathlessness, difficulty breathing and signs of heart failure, the pulse weakens, there may be cyanosis (a bluish tinge) of varying degree, and the baby's condition may progress to shock.<Cite n={1} /> Breathlessness can occur even at rest and gets worse during feeds; the baby gains weight slowly and often has respiratory infections.<Cite n={1} />

In a newborn, shock may be the first sign of the defect.<Cite n={3} /> The baby looks seriously ill: the mucous membranes are pale or bluish, the hands and feet are cold, the pulse is weak, blood pressure is low, and the baby responds poorly to touch.<Cite n={3} />

The difference in skin colour between the upper and lower halves of the body may be hardly noticeable: when there is a septal defect, it is usually slight or absent.<Cite n={1} />

</Patient>

<Student>

While the PDA is patent, the pulses in the arms and legs are equal; as the duct narrows, the pulse weakens in the lower limbs and, in type B, also in the left arm.<Cite n={1} />

There is no pathognomonic auscultatory picture: the systolic murmur of the VSD is absent or faint, and there may be a diastolic murmur over the pulmonary artery and an accentuated second heart sound due to pulmonary hypertension.<Cite n={1} /> The risk of necrotising enterocolitis is particularly high, and it worsens the prognosis.<Cite n={1} />

According to MSD Manuals, shock in a newborn can also be the first sign of other defects, such as [hypoplastic left heart syndrome](../hypoplastic-left-heart-syndrome/) and [coarctation of the aorta](../coarctation-of-the-aorta/).<Cite n={3} />

</Student>

## How it is diagnosed

<Patient>

The Russian Ministry of Health paediatric guideline provides for an ultrasound scan of the baby's heart in the womb to find the defect before birth, but late diagnosis is still possible.<Cite n={1} /> After birth, an exact diagnosis cannot be made from examination and listening to the heart alone.<Cite n={1} />

The main test is a heart ultrasound (echocardiography) with Doppler: it shows the interruption of the arch, its type and any associated defects.<Cite n={1} /> An ECG and a chest X-ray are also done, and if the ultrasound does not give enough information to plan the operation, a contrast CT scan of the heart.<Cite n={1} />

Because the defect is often linked to DiGeorge syndrome, if this syndrome is suspected the levels of calcium and parathyroid hormone in the blood are checked and genetic testing is done.<Cite n={1} />

</Patient>

<Student>

Echocardiography with colour flow Doppler is the main tool for diagnosis and for determining the type of interruption; it assesses the location and size of the VSD, subaortic obstruction, the arch anatomy and the degree of pulmonary hypertension, and shows a clear disproportion between the dilated pulmonary artery and the aorta.<Cite n={1} /> Echocardiography distinguishes an interrupted arch from coarctation of the aorta with tubular arch hypoplasia.<Cite n={1} />

The ECG usually shows signs of right rather than left ventricular hypertrophy.<Cite n={1} /> Cardiac catheterisation is not used routinely in isolated forms and is reserved for complex associated defects.<Cite n={1} />

</Student>

## How it is treated

<Patient>

The only way to correct the defect is surgery, and it is indicated for all children with an interrupted aortic arch.<Cite n={1} /> Newborns are operated on urgently; if the baby's condition is stable, the operation is done soon, but not as an emergency.<Cite n={1} />

Before the operation, doctors in hospital stabilise the baby's condition. If there are signs that the duct is closing, the baby is immediately given a medicine into a vein that keeps the duct from closing, and it is given continuously until the operation.<Cite n={1} /> The baby is usually also put on a ventilator.<Cite n={1} /> Heart failure medicines are only a "bridge" to the operation and do not replace it.<Cite n={1} />

The defect is usually repaired in a single operation using a heart-lung machine: the surgeon restores the continuity of the aortic arch, removes the duct tissue and closes the septal defect with a patch.<Cite n={1} /> The surgeon decides how to rebuild the arch according to the anatomy: from the aorta's own tissue or with additional materials.<Cite n={1} /> If one big operation is too risky, treatment is done in two stages.<Cite n={1} />

According to the Russian Ministry of Health paediatric guideline, after timely surgery the outlook is favourable in most cases.<Cite n={1} /> But the aortic arch can narrow again, and as the child grows the chance of a repeat procedure increases; it is often done through a blood vessel, without open surgery.<Cite n={1} /> Heart rhythm problems may also occur later.<Cite n={1} />

Follow-up is needed for life: until the age of 18 with a paediatric cardiologist, then with a cardiologist or a general physician; it includes an ECG, a heart ultrasound and 24-hour ECG recording.<Cite n={1} /> For adults with congenital heart disease, the ESC describes care as a lifelong process and recommends that each of them visit a specialist centre at least once.<Cite n={2} />

</Patient>

<Student>

In single-stage repair, according to the Russian Ministry of Health, after division of the aorta the ductal tissue is removed as completely as possible, and the arch is reconstructed with an end-to-side anastomosis or other techniques.<Cite n={1} /> With a long gap, homograft tissue or autologous pulmonary artery tissue may be used; the VSD is closed with a patch of synthetic or biological material.<Cite n={1} />

Two-stage treatment is recommended when single-stage repair is not possible, and a hybrid approach selectively in the presence of comorbidities, to avoid cardiopulmonary bypass.<Cite n={1} />

Residual or new obstruction of the neo-arch and of the left ventricular outflow tract is a known problem after repair.<Cite n={1} /> With haemodynamically significant obstruction, balloon dilatation is possible, either as a palliative measure whose effect is often short-lived or as a trial procedure before stent placement; in adults with suitable anatomy, stenting is recommended.<Cite n={1} /> According to the Russian Ministry of Health, a residual interventricular shunt occurs in up to 5% of cases.<Cite n={1} />

The ESC classes interrupted aortic arch, repaired or unrepaired, as congenital heart disease of severe complexity.<Cite n={2} />

</Student>

## When to call an ambulance

The Russian Ministry of Health paediatric guideline names an interrupted aortic arch found before or after birth as a reason for emergency admission to hospital.<Cite n={1} /> Heart failure or cyanosis in the first week of life is a medical emergency.<Cite n={4} />

Call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK) if a newborn's lips or skin turn pale or blue, the hands and feet become cold, the baby becomes floppy and responds poorly, or breathes with difficulty or struggles for breath during feeds.<Cite n={[1, 3, 4]} />

After the operation, an attack of heart rhythm disturbance with fainting or near-fainting is a reason for emergency admission to hospital: call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK).<Cite n={1} /> A few weeks after the operation a complication may develop that shows up as fever, tiredness, vomiting, or chest or abdominal pain; with such symptoms, see a doctor urgently.<Cite n={1} /> As with any heart condition, call your local emergency number if someone faints, has chest pain or is severely short of breath: this is the site's general safety rule, repeated at the bottom of every page.
