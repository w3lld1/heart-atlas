Pulmonary regurgitation, or pulmonary valve insufficiency, is a condition in which the <Structure id="pulmonary-valve">pulmonary valve</Structure> does not close completely, and during diastole some blood flows back from the <Structure id="pulmonary-trunk">pulmonary artery</Structure> into the <Structure id="right-ventricle">right ventricle</Structure>.<Cite n={[4, 5]} /> A very small, "trace" regurgitation is found in nearly everyone, especially in older people, and is considered physiological.<Cite n={6} /> Mild regurgitation is often found on a heart ultrasound and needs no intervention.<Cite n={4} />

## Why it happens

<Patient>

Pulmonary regurgitation is usually not a disease in its own right but a consequence of another one.<Cite n={6} /> In adults the most common cause is [pulmonary hypertension](../pulmonary-hypertension/): high pressure in the pulmonary arteries strains the valve, and it starts to let blood leak back.<Cite n={[4, 5]} />

Less often the cause is a congenital defect of the valve, an infection of the valve ([infective endocarditis](../infective-endocarditis/)), widening of the pulmonary artery or surgery for a heart defect.<Cite n={[4, 5]} /> An important group is people who had [tetralogy of Fallot](../tetralogy-of-fallot/) repaired in childhood: this operation widens the path from the right ventricle to the pulmonary artery, and, according to the European guideline, if the patch is placed across the valve ring, significant regurgitation is almost always present after surgery.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> Regurgitation can also appear or worsen after treatment of [pulmonary valve stenosis](../pulmonary-valve-stenosis/).<Cite n={[1, 3]} />

</Patient>

<Student>

The pulmonary valve has three semilunar leaflets of equal size; in systole they open completely, and in diastole they close completely, preventing backflow.<Cite n={7} /> StatPearls names dilation of the valve ring as the most common cause of regurgitation, and MSD names secondary pulmonary hypertension in adults.<Cite n={[4, 7]} /> Regurgitation is also a secondary finding in [dilated cardiomyopathy](../dilated-cardiomyopathy/).<Cite n={6} />

Less common causes are congenital heart disease, idiopathic pulmonary artery dilation, infective endocarditis, and surgical repair of tetralogy of Fallot or other defects involving the valve; rare causes are carcinoid syndrome, rheumatic fever, intervention for valvular stenosis, absent pulmonary valve syndrome and severe [Ebstein anomaly](../ebstein-anomaly/).<Cite n={4} /> Conduits between the right ventricle and the pulmonary artery have limited durability; their complications include progressive obstruction with or without regurgitation.<Cite n={1} />

Clinically significant regurgitation can present with right-sided volume overload and heart failure.<Cite n={6} /> Its harmful effects are augmented by stenoses of the pulmonary artery branches and by pulmonary hypertension.<Cite n={1} /> Dilation of the right ventricle can cause significant [tricuspid regurgitation](../tricuspid-regurgitation/), which dilates it further.<Cite n={1} /> When the cause is pulmonary hypertension, it is mainly the hypertension, not the regurgitation itself, that leads to right ventricular failure.<Cite n={4} /> Rarely, acute regurgitation in endocarditis causes acute right ventricular failure.<Cite n={4} />

</Student>

## Symptoms

<Patient>

Pulmonary regurgitation usually causes no symptoms.<Cite n={[4, 5]} /> Sometimes swollen ankles or tiredness appear.<Cite n={5} /> A few people develop signs of right ventricular failure: breathlessness, poor exercise tolerance, swelling, congestion of the liver.<Cite n={4} /> More about them in the article on [heart failure](../heart-failure/).

</Patient>

<Student>

The murmur of pulmonary regurgitation due to pulmonary hypertension is a high-pitched, early diastolic decrescendo murmur that begins with the pulmonary component of the second heart sound (P2) and ends before the first sound (Graham Steell murmur).<Cite n={4} /> Without pulmonary hypertension the murmur is shorter, lower-pitched and rougher, and begins after P2.<Cite n={4} /> Both murmurs resemble the murmur of [aortic regurgitation](../aortic-regurgitation/), but the murmur of pulmonary regurgitation usually gets louder on inspiration.<Cite n={4} /> After repair of tetralogy of Fallot, a low-pitched, early-ending diastolic murmur suggests severe regurgitation.<Cite n={1} />

</Student>

## How it is diagnosed

<Patient>

Regurgitation is often found by chance: from a heart murmur during an examination or during tests done for another reason.<Cite n={[4, 5]} /> It is confirmed with a [heart ultrasound (echocardiography)](../../diagnostic/echocardiography/), which shows the leaking valve and how much blood flows back, and this is used to judge its severity.<Cite n={5} /> An [ECG](../../diagnostic/ecg/) and a chest X-ray are usually done as well.<Cite n={[4, 5]} /> Cardiac MRI gives a more precise measure of the severity of regurgitation and the size of the right ventricle.<Cite n={[1, 4]} />

</Patient>

<Student>

Judging the severity of regurgitation by echocardiography can be difficult; cardiac MRI measures the regurgitant fraction and the degree of right ventricular dilation.<Cite n={4} /> After repair of tetralogy of Fallot, MRI is the method of choice for assessing right ventricular volume and function and regurgitation; the Russian Ministry of Health guideline also states that MRI is more informative than echocardiography for quantifying RV volumes and pulmonary regurgitation.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> The ESC guideline calls regurgitation severe when the regurgitant fraction on MRI is more than 30–40%; this is a definition of severity, not an indication for valve replacement on its own.<Cite n={1} />

The ECG may show signs of right ventricular hypertrophy; the chest X-ray may show RV enlargement and signs of the conditions causing pulmonary hypertension.<Cite n={4} /> After repair of tetralogy of Fallot, the ECG often shows complete [right bundle branch block](../bundle-branch-block/), depending on the surgical approach.<Cite n={1} /> Cardiopulmonary exercise testing helps choose the timing of reintervention.<Cite n={1} />

</Student>

## How it is treated

<Patient>

Treatment is aimed first at the cause, especially pulmonary hypertension; the regurgitation itself usually needs no specific treatment.<Cite n={4} /> The valve rarely has to be replaced, mainly in people with congenital heart disease who have already had a procedure on the valve.<Cite n={[4, 5]} /> Replacement is considered when the right ventricle dilates or weakens, especially if there are symptoms.<Cite n={4} />

The valve is replaced in one of two ways: by open surgery or through a catheter passed through a large vein, usually the femoral vein.<Cite n={[4, 8]} /> The catheter approach is used mainly in people who have previously had a conduit or a valve replacement.<Cite n={[1, 8]} /> The doctor chooses the method based on the person's characteristics and anatomy.<Cite n={4} /> Guidelines recommend that these procedures are done only in centres that specialise in congenital heart disease.<Cite n={[1, 8]} />

A biological valve wears out over time, and many young people may need several replacements during their lifetime.<Cite n={[1, 8]} /> After valve replacement there remains a risk of infective endocarditis.<Cite n={1} /> Tell the cardiologist in advance about dental procedures: endocarditis prevention may be needed.<Cite n={2} /> People who have had surgery for tetralogy of Fallot need regular follow-up in a specialised centre with regular cardiac MRI.<Cite n={1} />

</Patient>

<Student>

After repair of tetralogy of Fallot, the ESC recommends pulmonary valve replacement for severe regurgitation with symptoms (class I, level C).<Cite n={1} /> Without symptoms it should be considered, among other criteria, when objective exercise capacity declines, the RV shows progressive dilation or systolic dysfunction, or tricuspid regurgitation progresses to at least moderate (IIa, C).<Cite n={1} /> The Russian Ministry of Health guideline recommends valve replacement in older patients and adults after repair who have pulmonary valve insufficiency combined with RV dilation, tricuspid valve insufficiency or QRS widening; the RV volume thresholds in the Russian and ESC guidelines differ, so they are not given here.<Cite n={[1, 2]} />

The optimal timing of intervention is difficult to determine: trends over serial examinations matter more than a single measurement.<Cite n={1} /> A meta-analysis showed that valve replacement reduces symptoms and RV volume, but a survival benefit has not been shown; in patients without heart failure or advanced ventricular dysfunction, the mortality of the procedure is low.<Cite n={1} />

Transcatheter implantation has become an alternative to open surgery mainly for stenosis or regurgitation of a right ventricular outflow tract conduit, and in selected patients also for a native outflow tract.<Cite n={1} /> According to the ESC, a biological valve lasts 10–20 years on average, and the next replacement can be done by transcatheter valve-in-valve implantation.<Cite n={1} /> After repair of tetralogy of Fallot, other late complications are possible, including arrhythmias and sudden death; they are covered in the article on [tetralogy of Fallot](../tetralogy-of-fallot/).<Cite n={1} />

</Student>

## When to see a doctor urgently

Pulmonary regurgitation itself usually causes no symptoms and is well tolerated for years.<Cite n={[1, 4]} /> Rarely, in endocarditis, it develops suddenly and causes acute right ventricular failure.<Cite n={4} /> In its information for patients with tetralogy of Fallot, the Russian Ministry of Health guideline advises an unscheduled visit to a cardiologist as soon as possible if tiredness, breathlessness, bluish skin, swelling, a growing abdomen, heart rhythm disturbances or loss of consciousness appear or rapidly get worse, and also with a fever of unknown cause.<Cite n={2} />

But for fainting, chest pain or severe breathlessness, do not wait for a cardiologist appointment: call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK). This is the site's general safety rule. When else emergency help is needed is explained in the articles on [ventricular tachycardia](../ventricular-tachycardia/), [infective endocarditis](../infective-endocarditis/) and [heart failure](../heart-failure/).
