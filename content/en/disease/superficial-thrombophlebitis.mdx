Superficial thrombophlebitis (superficial vein thrombosis) is a condition in which a blood clot forms in a vein under the skin, and the vein wall, the fatty tissue under the skin and the skin around it become inflamed.<Cite n={1} /> It most often affects the veins of the legs.<Cite n={5} /> The most common risk factor is [varicose veins](../varicose-veins/); thrombophlebitis of a varicose vein is called varicothrombophlebitis.<Cite n={1} /> The greatest danger is that the clot spreads into the deep veins ([deep vein thrombosis](../deep-vein-thrombosis/)) and causes [pulmonary embolism (PE)](../pulmonary-embolism/).<Cite n={[1, 4]} />

## Why it happens

<Patient>

Thrombophlebitis most often develops in varicose veins of the legs.<Cite n={1} /> But most people with varicose veins never get clots in their veins.<Cite n={5} /> Thrombophlebitis also occurs in ordinary veins that are not varicose.<Cite n={1} />

The risk is also raised by age, excess weight, smoking (including e-cigarettes and heated tobacco products), previous thromboses, pregnancy and the first month after giving birth, hormonal contraceptive pills and hormone replacement therapy, immobility, recent operations and injuries, and cancer.<Cite n={[1, 4]} /> In the arms, thrombophlebitis usually results from a drip, that is, an intravenous (IV) catheter.<Cite n={[4, 5]} />

A clot in a superficial vein itself rarely breaks off: the inflammation attaches it firmly to the vein wall, and superficial veins have no surrounding muscles that could squeeze and dislodge it.<Cite n={5} /> The danger lies elsewhere: the clot can spread into the deep veins.<Cite n={1} />

</Patient>

<Student>

The Russian Ministry of Health guideline distinguishes varicothrombophlebitis from thrombosis of non-varicose superficial veins: prognosis and management differ substantially between them.<Cite n={1} /> In thrombosis of non-varicose superficial veins, DVT and PE are found at diagnosis more often than in varicothrombophlebitis; when there are signs suggesting possible cancer, the Russian Ministry of Health guideline recommends referring the patient to an internist, a general practitioner or an oncologist to decide whether a search for cancer is needed.<Cite n={1} />

Migratory thrombophlebitis is an involvement of the superficial veins in which the process subsides in some areas and arises in others, often distant ones; the thrombi themselves do not move.<Cite n={1} /> It may be a harbinger of pancreatic cancer and other adenocarcinomas (Trousseau syndrome).<Cite n={[4, 5]} />

According to estimates cited by the Russian Ministry of Health guideline, superficial thrombophlebitis is diagnosed in about 1 in 1,000 people each year (data from studies in the USA and the Netherlands); it is more common in women, and its incidence rises with age.<Cite n={1} /> The true prevalence may be higher: many patients with limited forms do not seek medical help.<Cite n={1} />

</Student>

## Symptoms

<Patient>

Thrombophlebitis shows itself as a painful lump with redness and local warmth of the skin over varicose veins or a large superficial vein; sometimes a firm, tender cord can be felt.<Cite n={1} /> Pain and swelling develop quickly.<Cite n={5} /> The vein with the clot feels like a hard cord under the skin, not soft like a normal or varicose vein.<Cite n={5} /> On dark skin, the redness may look purplish or be hard to see.<Cite n={5} /> Sometimes there is general malaise, chills and a raised temperature.<Cite n={1} />

The inflammation usually subsides within a few days, but the lumps and tenderness may last for weeks.<Cite n={5} /> According to the Russian Ministry of Health guideline, lumps where the veins were clotted, and symptoms, sometimes persist for six months or longer; no special rehabilitation is needed after thrombophlebitis.<Cite n={1} />

</Patient>

<Student>

The clinical picture is determined by local inflammation in a limited segment of a superficial vein: pain, general malaise, low-grade fever, local erythema, oedema and a tender induration along the vein.<Cite n={1} /> On palpation, the doctor looks for a firm, cord-like, markedly tender strand or indurated varicose nodules, and for local warmth (hyperthermia).<Cite n={[1, 4]} />

The Russian Ministry of Health guideline divides superficial vein thrombosis into stages: acute (0–7 days from onset), subsiding (1–3 weeks) and subsided (more than 3 weeks: no inflammation, but painless or mildly painful indurations remain, and skin hyperpigmentation is possible).<Cite n={1} /> The division is approximate: the doctor determines the stage by the severity and course of the inflammation.<Cite n={1} />

The risk of the thrombus spreading into the deep veins is determined primarily by the proximal end of the thrombus.<Cite n={1} /> Low risk is isolated thrombophlebitis of tributaries of the main superficial veins; moderate risk is thrombophlebitis of the trunk of the great, small or anterior accessory saphenous vein or of tributaries near the junction, with the thrombus ending more than 3 cm from the junction; high risk is a thrombus ending at that distance or closer.<Cite n={1} />

</Student>

## How it is diagnosed

<Patient>

The doctor asks questions, examines and feels both legs and looks for signs of deep vein thrombosis and PE.<Cite n={1} /> The main test is an ultrasound scan of the veins (duplex scanning).<Cite n={1} /> According to the Russian Ministry of Health guideline, it is done whenever thrombophlebitis is suspected, and it looks at the superficial and deep veins of both legs.<Cite n={1} /> The scan shows where the upper end of the clot is: the closer it is to the junction, the place where the superficial vein joins a deep one, the higher the risk that the clot will spread into the deep veins.<Cite n={[1, 4]} />

The British NICE guideline on varicose veins advises referring people with superficial vein thrombosis (hard, painful veins) to a vascular service when venous incompetence is suspected.<Cite n={3} /> The Russian Ministry of Health guideline does not recommend laboratory tests for diagnosis or for choosing treatment.<Cite n={1} />

</Patient>

<Student>

The diagnostic criterion is the combination of signs of soft-tissue inflammation on history and examination with ultrasound signs of thrombotic occlusion of a superficial vein in the inflamed area.<Cite n={1} /> Duplex scanning assesses the nature of the thrombus, its distal and proximal ends and the patency of the deep and perforator veins; the proximal extent of involvement must be stated precisely.<Cite n={1} />

The Russian Ministry of Health guideline does not recommend laboratory tests for diagnosis or for choosing management (this refers to screening and global coagulation tests), and markers of thrombus formation, including D-dimer, cannot fully determine the activity of the process or the probability of PE.<Cite n={1} /> Testing for inherited thrombophilia is not recommended if the result will not change thromboprophylaxis for the patient or their relatives.<Cite n={1} /> If the thrombus has spread into the deep veins, or DVT is found at the same time, the diagnosis is stated as deep vein thrombosis.<Cite n={1} />

</Student>

## How it is treated

<Patient>

The goals of treatment are to stop the clot from spreading into the deep veins and causing PE, to stop it from spreading along the superficial veins, to relieve the inflammation and symptoms, and to prevent thrombophlebitis from coming back.<Cite n={1} /> The doctor decides how to treat according to the stage of the disease and how high the risk is that the clot will spread into the deep veins.<Cite n={1} />

Treatment uses non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) as tablets or applied to the skin, compression hosiery and, in some situations, anticoagulants, medicines that reduce blood clotting.<Cite n={[1, 4]} /> Compression helps the vein open up again faster.<Cite n={1} /> Only a doctor chooses the medicine, the dose and the duration. Antibiotics are not needed for ordinary thrombophlebitis; they are given when suppurative (septic) thrombophlebitis is suspected, and then treatment is in hospital.<Cite n={1} />

Treatment is given as an outpatient or in hospital; people are admitted to hospital when the risk of the clot spreading into the deep veins is high in the acute stage, when the care needed cannot be given outside hospital, and in suppurative thrombophlebitis.<Cite n={1} /> If the clot has already spread into the deep veins, it is treated as [deep vein thrombosis](../deep-vein-thrombosis/).<Cite n={1} />

Even after the inflammation has gone, the risk of another thrombosis and of PE stays higher than usual for several years.<Cite n={1} /> In people with varicose veins, to prevent a first or repeated thrombophlebitis, the Russian Ministry of Health guideline recommends planned treatment to eliminate the varicose veins; after thrombophlebitis, this is done once it has subsided.<Cite n={1} />

Thrombophlebitis is rare during pregnancy but is dangerous because of thromboembolic complications; some medicines are usually not given to pregnant or breastfeeding women, so treatment is chosen by the doctor.<Cite n={1} />

</Patient>

<Student>

According to the Russian Ministry of Health guideline, at low risk in the acute and subsiding stages systemic NSAIDs are recommended (level of evidence 2, grade of recommendation B); at moderate risk, anticoagulant therapy is considered the preferred method (level of evidence 2, grade of recommendation A); at high risk, anticoagulants at therapeutic doses, as in DVT (level of evidence 5, grade of recommendation C).<Cite n={1} /> In the acute and subsiding stages, regardless of risk level, the Russian Ministry of Health guideline recommends elastic compression to speed up recanalisation of the thrombosed veins (level of evidence 2, grade of recommendation B).<Cite n={1} />

Routine use of antibacterial drugs in superficial vein thrombosis is not recommended; when suppurative thrombophlebitis is suspected, hospital admission and early empirical antibacterial therapy are indicated.<Cite n={1} /> If duplex scanning cannot be done, the Russian Ministry of Health guideline recommends admitting a patient with acute SVT to hospital when the signs of inflammation reach the middle third of the thigh in the great saphenous vein system or the upper third of the calf in the small saphenous vein system.<Cite n={1} />

Surgical methods (ligation at the junction, crossectomy) do not replace anticoagulant therapy; they are chosen at high risk of the thrombus spreading into the deep veins when full anticoagulation is not possible.<Cite n={1} /> The risk of recurrent VTE is highest in the first 3 months and remains above that of the general population for several years.<Cite n={1} />

</Student>

## When to call an ambulance

The Russian Ministry of Health guideline classes superficial vein thrombosis of the legs as a life-threatening condition for which people with varicose vein disease receive emergency medical care.<Cite n={2} /> If a painful, red lump or a firm cord appears along a vein, seek medical help urgently or call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK): an ultrasound scan of the superficial and deep veins of both legs is needed.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> Very severe inflammation together with a high temperature may mean suppurative thrombophlebitis; when it is suspected, the Russian Ministry of Health guideline recommends hospital admission.<Cite n={1} /> If, during treatment, the redness and lump do not get smaller, or they spread further along the vein, see a doctor again: in this case the Russian Ministry of Health guideline recommends repeating the ultrasound scan.<Cite n={1} />

A clot from a superficial vein can spread into the deep veins and cause PE.<Cite n={1} /> If the whole calf or leg is swollen and painful and the skin over it is warm, this may be deep vein thrombosis: seek medical help urgently or call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK).<Cite n={6} /> For more about the signs, see the articles on [deep vein thrombosis](../deep-vein-thrombosis/) and [pulmonary embolism](../pulmonary-embolism/). As everywhere on the site: call an ambulance for fainting, chest pain or severe shortness of breath; this is the general safety rule, repeated at the bottom of every page.
