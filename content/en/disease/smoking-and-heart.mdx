Smoking is a strong and independent risk factor for diseases of the heart and blood vessels.<Cite n={1} /> Tobacco harms nearly every organ in the body and causes, among other things, heart disease and stroke.<Cite n={[4, 7]} /> All forms of tobacco are harmful, and there is no safe level of exposure.<Cite n={4} />

This article is about what smoking does to the heart and blood vessels and how quitting helps.

## Why it happens

<Patient>

The harm of smoking comes mainly from breathing in smoke, while nicotine is mainly what causes the habit and the dependence.<Cite n={2} /> Most smoking-related health problems are caused by other substances in tobacco smoke, not by nicotine.<Cite n={3} /> Tobacco smoke contains thousands of chemicals, carbon monoxide among them.<Cite n={2} /> Nicotine itself is not harmless either: it slightly raises the heart rate and blood pressure and increases the risk of heart and blood vessel disease.<Cite n={[4, 7]} />

Smoking damages the inner lining of the arteries, raises blood pressure and heart rate for a while, makes the blood more prone to clotting and worsens the blood fats.<Cite n={7} /> Coronary heart disease is one of the main causes of tobacco-related deaths.<Cite n={7} /> Smoking also raises the risk of other blood vessel diseases, such as stroke and [aortic aneurysm](../abdominal-aortic-aneurysm/).<Cite n={7} />

Other people's smoke is dangerous too. There is no safe level of second-hand smoke: it causes serious heart and blood vessel diseases, including coronary heart disease.<Cite n={4} /> Cigarettes are not the only risk: some kinds of smokeless tobacco are also linked to heart and blood vessel disease.<Cite n={[1, 3, 7]} />

</Patient>

<Student>

Russian Ministry of Health guideline: tobacco smoke contains about 7,000 chemicals.<Cite n={2} /> ESC 2021: smoking causes 50% of all avoidable deaths in smokers, half of them due to ASCVD; a lifelong smoker has a 50% probability of dying from smoking and loses on average 10 years of life.<Cite n={1} /> Cardiovascular risk in smokers under 50 years is five times higher than in non-smokers; long-term smoking is more hazardous for women than for men.<Cite n={1} /> MSD: the risk of MI is doubled when smoking 1 pack a day, and the risk of cardiovascular mortality is increased by more than 50% over a 35-year period.<Cite n={7} />

According to the Russian Ministry of Health guideline, inflammatory processes and changes in the immune system are crucial in the pathogenesis of smoking-related diseases, atherosclerosis among them; cigarette smoke can also cause insulin resistance and the metabolic syndrome.<Cite n={2} />

Passive smoking is associated with an increased risk of CVD.<Cite n={1} /> According to MSD, in spouses of people who smoke, the risk of coronary artery disease is increased on average by approximately 20–30%.<Cite n={7} /> WHO estimates that second-hand smoke kills more than 1.6 million people prematurely every year.<Cite n={4} /> In Russia, according to 2023 Rosstat data cited by the Russian Ministry of Health guideline, 21.1% of the population used tobacco: 34.6% of men and 10.1% of women (not counting electronic nicotine delivery systems).<Cite n={2} />

</Student>

## Symptoms

<Patient>

The complaints of a person who smokes are usually the symptoms of the lung and heart diseases that smoking has caused.<Cite n={7} /> A chronic cough and shortness of breath on exertion are common; the person tolerates exercise less well, moves less, and their fitness drops even further.<Cite n={7} /> The signs of heart disease are described in the articles on [coronary heart disease](../coronary-heart-disease/) and [myocardial infarction](../myocardial-infarction/).

Smoking is a form of dependence.<Cite n={[2, 7]} /> When a person tries to quit, withdrawal symptoms appear: a strong craving to smoke, anxiety, irritability, trouble concentrating, poor sleep and increased appetite.<Cite n={[2, 8]} /> These feelings are why many people find it hard to stay off cigarettes even when they know the risks.<Cite n={8} /> Withdrawal is temporary.<Cite n={2} />

</Patient>

<Student>

According to the Russian Ministry of Health guideline, tobacco withdrawal also includes malaise and weakness, dysphoric mood, increased cough and aphthous stomatitis, and lasts from 7 days to several weeks, rarely months.<Cite n={2} />

According to MSD, withdrawal symptoms are worst in the first 3 days, when most people trying to quit relapse, and usually subside within 2–4 weeks, but craving may continue for months or even years.<Cite n={8} />

</Student>

## How it is diagnosed

<Patient>

Doctors are advised to ask every patient about smoking at every visit, whatever the reason for the visit.<Cite n={[7, 8]} /> The doctor asks at what age the person started, how much and for how long they smoke, which products they use and whether they have tried to quit.<Cite n={2} /> The strength of the dependence is assessed with one questionnaire, and readiness to quit with another.<Cite n={2} /> Carbon monoxide in the breath can be measured: it shows how heavily the person smokes, helps motivate quitting and later confirms that smoking has really stopped.<Cite n={2} />

Smoking is one of the main risk factors for heart and blood vessel disease, so in a person who smokes the doctor assesses the overall cardiovascular risk, taking into account other factors as well: blood pressure, cholesterol and diabetes.<Cite n={1} /> More about them in the articles on [hypertension](../hypertension/), [dyslipidaemia](../dyslipidemia/) and [diabetes and the heart](../diabetes-and-heart/).

</Patient>

<Student>

Russian Ministry of Health guideline: clinical diagnosis (complaints, history and the Fagerström test) is the main tool; tobacco dependence syndrome is diagnosed by ICD-10 when 3 or more of 6 features are present over 12 months, among them withdrawal, increased tolerance and continued use despite clear evidence of harm.<Cite n={2} />

Readiness to quit is assessed with the Prochaska questionnaire and smoking intensity with the smoking index (SI = cigarettes per day × years of smoking / 20); patients with an SI above 5 find it harder to quit on their own.<Cite n={2} /> The Russian Ministry of Health guideline classes exhaled CO of 6 to 10 ppm as light smoking, 11 to 20 ppm as moderate and more than 21 ppm as heavy.<Cite n={2} /> According to MSD, how soon a person smokes after waking also shows how severe the dependence is: smoking within 30 minutes is a sign of addiction.<Cite n={7} />

ESC 2021: systematic global CV risk assessment is recommended in individuals with any major vascular risk factor, smoking included (class I, level C).<Cite n={1} />

</Student>

## How it is treated

<Patient>

Quitting smoking is potentially the most effective of all preventive measures: it substantially reduces the rate of heart attacks, including repeat ones, and of deaths.<Cite n={1} /> It is worthwhile at any age.<Cite n={1} /> People with heart and blood vessel disease need to stop, especially during the acute phase of the illness.<Cite n={2} />

According to WHO, the benefit begins in the first minutes and hours after the last cigarette and grows over the years: heart rate and blood pressure drop, the carbon monoxide level in the blood returns to normal, and the risk of coronary heart disease gradually falls to that of a non-smoker.<Cite n={5} /> WHO also reports that people who quit after a heart attack markedly reduce their chances of having another one.<Cite n={5} /> The risk falls gradually: even in people who smoked heavily it decreases over several years but stays raised for some time.<Cite n={1} />

Stopping in one go is the best approach.<Cite n={3} /> Simply smoking fewer cigarettes may not improve health: people usually inhale more deeply to get their usual amount of nicotine.<Cite n={8} /> Weight often goes up after quitting, but this does not reduce the benefit for the heart and blood vessels, so quitting is still worthwhile.<Cite n={1} />

You do not have to quit alone. A doctor can give brief advice and offer help; behavioural support, motivational interviewing and cognitive behavioural therapy are used.<Cite n={[1, 2, 3]} /> Counselling together with medicines prescribed by a doctor can markedly increase the chance of quitting.<Cite n={4} /> Medicines reduce the discomfort of quitting while the person rebuilds their life without tobacco.<Cite n={8} /> Which medicines suit you is best discussed with a doctor, especially if you have heart disease. The Russian Ministry of Health guideline advises against methods without proven effectiveness for treating dependence, such as acupuncture, hypnosis, herbal remedies and homeopathy.<Cite n={2} />

**E-cigarettes and heated tobacco.** The sources differ here, so the position of each is given:

- WHO: e-cigarettes are harmful to health and are not safe.<Cite n={4} /> Their long-term effects are not yet fully known, but they generate toxic substances, some of which increase the risk of heart and lung disorders; the safest approach is to use neither tobacco nor e-cigarettes.<Cite n={6} />
- The Russian Ministry of Health guideline does not recommend e-cigarettes or smokeless tobacco products as a way to treat tobacco dependence, because there is no scientifically sound evidence of their effect on health.<Cite n={2} />
- ESC: e-cigarettes are probably less harmful than tobacco, but their long-term effects on the heart, blood vessels and lungs need further research; using both kinds of cigarettes at the same time should be avoided.<Cite n={1} />
- NICE (UK) includes nicotine-containing e-cigarettes among the options for adults to stop smoking and states that there is not enough evidence to know whether they have long-term harms, that they are likely to be substantially less harmful than smoking, and that any smoking is harmful, so people using e-cigarettes should stop smoking tobacco completely.<Cite n={3} />
- Heated tobacco products contain tobacco, so ESC does not recommend their use; WHO states that they are tobacco products and do not help people stop smoking.<Cite n={[1, 4]} />

</Patient>

<Student>

WHO: within 20 minutes of quitting, heart rate and blood pressure drop; at 12 hours, the blood carbon monoxide level drops to normal; at 1 year, the risk of CHD is about half that of a smoker; at 15 years, it is that of a non-smoker; people who quit after a heart attack reduce their chances of another heart attack by 50%.<Cite n={5} />

ESC 2021: cessation of all smoking is recommended, as smoking is a strong and independent risk factor for CVD (class I, level A); smoking cessation is recommended regardless of weight gain, as weight gain does not lessen the ASCVD benefits of cessation (class I, level B).<Cite n={1} /> According to ESC, even in people smoking more than 20 cigarettes a day, stopping reduces CV risk within 5 years, although the risk stays raised beyond 5 years.<Cite n={1} />

ESC very brief advice (Table 9) has three elements: ASK (establish and record smoking status), ADVISE (on the best way to stop) and ACT (offer help).<Cite n={1} /> NICE: offer behavioural support to people who smoke regardless of which option they choose to help them stop.<Cite n={3} />

WHO: counselling and medication can more than double a tobacco user's chance of successful quitting.<Cite n={4} /> According to MSD, combining counselling and medications is more effective than either alone.<Cite n={8} /> ESC: drug support for cessation should be considered in all smokers who are willing; three of the four cessation medicines it lists do not increase the risk of serious cardiovascular adverse events during or after treatment.<Cite n={1} /> Russian Ministry of Health guideline: patients at any cardiovascular risk should receive instructions on quitting, and therapy is chosen after weighing risks and benefits.<Cite n={2} />

E-cigarettes: ESC notes that they are addictive, so their use should be subject to the same marketing controls as standard cigarettes.<Cite n={1} /> According to MSD, the health benefits of e-cigarettes in the context of dual use are unproven.<Cite n={8} /> WHO: e-cigarette use increases conventional cigarette uptake, particularly among non-smoking youth, by nearly 3 times; dual use is at least as dangerous as, and likely more dangerous than, smoking conventional cigarettes or using e-cigarettes alone.<Cite n={6} />

</Student>

## When to call an ambulance

Smoking itself does not need emergency care; the danger lies in the diseases it leads to: heart attack and stroke.<Cite n={[4, 7]} /> For chest pain or pressure, severe shortness of breath, fainting or signs of a stroke, call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK) at once: this is the site's general safety rule. The signs of a heart attack are described in the article on [myocardial infarction](../myocardial-infarction/), and the signs of a stroke in the article on [carotid artery stenosis](../carotid-artery-stenosis/).
