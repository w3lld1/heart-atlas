Marfan syndrome is an inherited disorder of connective tissue. It is caused by changes (pathogenic variants) in the FBN1 gene, which encodes the protein fibrillin-1.<Cite n={[1, 4]} /> The condition affects the skeleton, eyes, lungs, skin, nervous system, heart and blood vessels, but its most dangerous complications involve the aorta: its first segment, the aortic root, gradually widens, and there is a risk of [aortic dissection](../aortic-dissection/).<Cite n={[1, 4]} />

The American guideline on aortic disease (ACC/AHA) estimates that Marfan syndrome affects about 1 in 5,000 people; the MSD Manual gives lower estimates.<Cite n={[1, 4]} />

## Why it happens

<Patient>

Fibrillin-1 is the main protein of microfibrils, the fine fibres of connective tissue; it helps anchor cells to the material around them.<Cite n={4} /> In Marfan syndrome the middle layer of the wall of the aortic root and ascending aorta is affected most, in the places where the blood flow puts the most stress on it.<Cite n={4} /> The aorta gradually widens, usually starting at the sinuses, sometimes before the age of 10.<Cite n={4} />

The condition is inherited: a child of a person with Marfan syndrome has a 50% chance of having it.<Cite n={1} /> In about a quarter of patients the gene change is new, so no one in the family had the condition before.<Cite n={4} />

</Patient>

<Student>

Marfan syndrome is an autosomal dominant, highly penetrant, progressive condition.<Cite n={4} /> The aortic root is most often dilated, followed by the ascending aorta; effacement of the sinotubular junction with enlargement of the proximal ascending aorta is often present.<Cite n={1} /> Descending and abdominal aortic aneurysms are less common; type B aortic dissection is the initial aortic event in about 10% of patients and may also occur after root replacement.<Cite n={1} />

Neonatal Marfan syndrome is a severe form that presents before 1 year of age with significant valvular disease and heart failure.<Cite n={4} />

</Student>

## Symptoms

<Patient>

Marfan syndrome is usually recognised by a combination of long arms and legs, a widened aortic root and a displaced lens of the eye.<Cite n={4} /> People with the syndrome are taller than others of their age and than their relatives, their arm span is greater than their height, and their fingers are long and thin.<Cite n={4} /> The breastbone may be sunken or pushed outward, the joints are very flexible, and flat feet and a curved spine are common.<Cite n={4} /> The eyes may have a displaced lens, severe short-sightedness or retinal detachment, and the lungs may develop cysts and a spontaneous pneumothorax (collapsed lung), which can cause chest pain and breathlessness.<Cite n={4} />

In many cases a widened aorta causes no symptoms at all, so it is looked for during check-ups.<Cite n={3} /> Because the aortic root is widened, the aortic valve may start to let blood leak back; this is [aortic regurgitation](../aortic-regurgitation/).<Cite n={4} /> The mitral valve leaflets may bulge back into the left atrium (mitral valve prolapse), and the valve may let blood leak backward ([mitral regurgitation](../mitral-regurgitation/)).<Cite n={4} /> [Infective endocarditis](../infective-endocarditis/) can develop on the affected valves.<Cite n={4} /> The main life-threatening complication is aortic dissection.<Cite n={4} />

</Patient>

<Student>

The major cardiovascular findings are aortic root dilation, ascending aortic aneurysm or aortic dissection, and valvular prolapse, typically mitral.<Cite n={4} /> Most patients, including children, have aortic root dilation; it can cause aortic regurgitation with a diastolic decrescendo murmur.<Cite n={4} /> Mitral valve prolapse may cause a midsystolic click, and a late systolic or holosystolic murmur signifies regurgitation.<Cite n={4} /> Marfan syndrome is one of the causes of myxomatous degeneration of the mitral valve.<Cite n={5} /> Aortic root dilation and type A aortic dissection are the leading causes of morbidity and mortality in Marfan syndrome.<Cite n={1} />

Ocular findings include ectopia lentis (subluxation or upward dislocation of the lens) and iridodonesis; dural ectasia is common, most often in the lumbosacral spine, and may cause headache and lower back pain.<Cite n={4} />

</Student>

## How it is diagnosed

<Patient>

The diagnosis is based on clinical features and genetic testing.<Cite n={4} /> Doctors use the modified Ghent criteria, which take into account the genetic test result, a points total of physical and other features (the systemic score), a displaced lens and the condition in relatives.<Cite n={1} />

The size of the aortic root is measured with a [heart ultrasound (echocardiography)](../../diagnostic/echocardiography/) or a heart MRI; an eye specialist examines the eyes; some features need X-rays of the spine and pelvis and an MRI of the lower spine.<Cite n={4} /> A genetic test helps when the features are not enough for a diagnosis, but a negative result does not rule the condition out.<Cite n={4} /> If a gene change is found, testing of blood relatives who may carry it is recommended, whatever their age.<Cite n={2} /> Assessment in a centre experienced in inherited aortic disease is recommended.<Cite n={2} />

</Patient>

<Student>

In MSD's simplified summary, the diagnosis is made with aortic root dilation plus an FBN1 mutation, ectopia lentis or a positive systemic score; with an FBN1 mutation plus ectopia lentis; or with a family history plus ectopia lentis, aortic root dilation or a positive systemic score.<Cite n={4} /> The systemic score includes, among others, the wrist and thumb signs, chest wall deformities, pes planus, spontaneous pneumothorax, dural ectasia, scoliosis, skin striae, severe myopia and mitral valve prolapse; in the MSD table a score of 7 points or more is considered positive.<Cite n={4} /> Aortic root diameter is indexed to body size (Z-score); without a family history, dilation means Z ≥ 2.<Cite n={4} />

The differential diagnosis includes Loeys–Dietz syndrome, vascular Ehlers–Danlos syndrome, hereditary thoracic aortic disease and congenital contractural arachnodactyly; homocystinuria is distinguished by detecting homocystine in the urine.<Cite n={4} />

ACC/AHA recommend transthoracic echocardiography at diagnosis and 6 months later to determine the rate of aortic growth, then annually if the diameters are stable; if the aortic root or ascending aorta is not adequately visualised, CT or MRI is performed.<Cite n={1} /> Per the ESC and Russian Ministry of Health guidelines, echocardiography is done at least annually when the root diameter is below 45 mm without additional risk factors, and more often with risk factors or a larger diameter; at the first evaluation of patients without previous aortic surgery, the whole aorta and peripheral arteries are imaged (CT or MRI, and ultrasound).<Cite n={[2, 3]} />

</Student>

## How it is treated

<Patient>

Treatment aims to prevent and treat complications, above all aortic dissection.<Cite n={4} /> The heart, bones and eyes are checked every year.<Cite n={4} />

To slow the widening of the aorta, guidelines suggest medicines from the beta-blocker or angiotensin II receptor blocker groups.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> Treatment is tailored to each person and decided together with them; which medicines are needed is decided by the doctor.<Cite n={2} />

When the aortic root becomes too wide, it is replaced together with the ascending aorta by a graft in advance, before a dissection happens: this planned operation prevents dissection and improves survival.<Cite n={1} /> Doctors decide when to operate based on the size of the aorta, how fast it grows, age, body size, dissection in relatives and other individual factors.<Cite n={1} /> If the anatomy of the valve allows it and an experienced surgeon is available, the patient's own aortic valve is preserved.<Cite n={2} /> A displaced lens and retinal detachment are corrected with surgery; a curved spine is treated with a brace, and with surgery if the curve is severe.<Cite n={4} />

Physical activity is chosen individually; regular moderate aerobic exercise is recommended for most people.<Cite n={2} /> Especially when the aorta is widened, exercising at peak intensity, lifting heavy weights while straining, and sports with a risk of collision are discouraged.<Cite n={[1, 4]} />

Pregnancy changes the circulation and hormones and raises the risk of dissection in women with aortic disease; dissection happens most often in the third trimester or in the first 12 weeks after delivery.<Cite n={1} /> For this reason all women with Marfan syndrome are advised to have an evaluation before conception and follow-up in a centre with access to a pregnancy heart and vessel team, and couples are offered genetic counselling before conception.<Cite n={2} /> Decisions about pregnancy are made together with the doctors.<Cite n={1} />

With treatment and regular monitoring, life expectancy in Marfan syndrome approaches that of the general population.<Cite n={4} />

</Patient>

<Student>

A beta-blocker or an angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) to reduce the rate of aortic dilation is recommended by ESC and ACC/AHA as class I, and their combination as class IIa (ESC) or 2a (ACC/AHA).<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> Randomised trials comparing an ARB with a beta-blocker found no significant difference in aortic root growth or clinical events.<Cite n={1} />

ESC: surgery is indicated at a maximal aortic sinus diameter ≥ 50 mm (I B) and should be considered at ≥ 45 mm with additional risk factors (IIa C).<Cite n={2} /> The ACC/AHA and Russian Ministry of Health guidelines give the same thresholds: 5.0 cm, and 4.5 cm with risk factors.<Cite n={[1, 3]} /> Features associated with an increased risk of aortic complications per the ACC/AHA and Russian Ministry of Health guidelines: family history of aortic dissection, rapid aortic growth (≥ 0.3 cm/year), diffuse aortic root and ascending aortic dilation, and marked vertebral arterial tortuosity.<Cite n={[1, 3]} /> Root replacement uses a composite valved graft conduit (modified Bentall procedure) or valve-sparing root replacement (David procedure); ESC recommends the valve-sparing technique when the anatomy of the valve allows its preservation and the surgeon has specific expertise (I B).<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> After root replacement, surveillance imaging of the thoracic aorta (MRI or CT) continues.<Cite n={[1, 2]} />

</Student>

## When to call an ambulance

Marfan syndrome is a high-risk condition for aortic dissection.<Cite n={1} /> Call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK) for sudden, severe pain in the chest or back, sometimes in the abdomen.<Cite n={1} /> The classic description is tearing or ripping pain, but more often it is described as sharp or stabbing and is worst at the very start.<Cite n={1} /> More about the warning signs is in the article on [aortic dissection](../aortic-dissection/).

A spontaneous pneumothorax can also cause chest pain and breathlessness.<Cite n={4} /> As with any heart condition, call an ambulance for fainting, chest pain or severe shortness of breath: this is the site's general safety rule, repeated at the bottom of every page.
