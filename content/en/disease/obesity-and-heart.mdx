Obesity is a chronic disease in which excess fat tissue builds up in the body. It is one of the main risk factors for type 2 diabetes and for diseases of the heart and blood vessels.<Cite n={2} /> Obesity results from complex interactions between living conditions, psychological and social influences and biological susceptibility.<Cite n={8} /> It is not solely an individual responsibility: eating and physical activity are strongly shaped by the environment and society, which often limit the choices people have.<Cite n={8} />

This article is about what obesity does to the heart and blood vessels. The related conditions are covered in more detail in the articles on [high blood pressure](../hypertension/), [dyslipidaemia](../dyslipidemia/), [diabetes and the heart](../diabetes-and-heart/), [heart failure](../heart-failure/) and [atrial fibrillation](../atrial-fibrillation/).

## Why it happens

<Patient>

Obesity has many causes. Besides the gap between how much energy a person takes in with food and how much they use, hormonal and nervous regulation and living conditions play a part.<Cite n={2} /> High-calorie food and an inactive lifestyle are among the most important causes.<Cite n={2} /> Genes affect appetite and where body fat is stored.<Cite n={10} />

Visceral fat, the fat inside the abdomen around the organs, is particularly important for the heart (it is shown in the diagram above); this fat is more harmful than fat under the skin.<Cite n={[1, 10]} /> Obesity affects the heart and blood vessels in several ways:

- it raises blood pressure; when weight goes down, blood pressure goes down too, and blood pressure medicines work better;<Cite n={6} />
- it disturbs the levels of cholesterol and other blood fats, which speeds up atherosclerosis and raises the risk of coronary heart disease and stroke;<Cite n={[9, 10]} />
- it raises the risk of type 2 diabetes;<Cite n={2} />
- it raises the risk of heart failure;<Cite n={[7, 10]} />
- it raises the risk of atrial fibrillation.<Cite n={[1, 4, 5]} />

Obesity is often accompanied by obstructive sleep apnoea, pauses in breathing during sleep: excess fat in the neck can press on the airway.<Cite n={[9, 10]} /> Sleep apnoea itself raises the risk of high blood pressure, heart rhythm problems, heart attack, heart failure and stroke.<Cite n={10} />

</Patient>

<Student>

WHO: in 2024, 1 in 7 people in the world (1.1 billion) were living with obesity; among adults aged 18 years and older, the figure was about 16%. The worldwide prevalence of obesity more than doubled between 1990 and 2024.<Cite n={8} /> According to the ESSE-RF study cited in the Russian Ministry of Health guideline, the prevalence of obesity among adults aged 25–64 years in 11 regions of Russia was 29.7%.<Cite n={2} />

ESC 2021: obesity increases CVD risk both through the major traditional risk factors and through other mechanisms; its main consequences are hypertension, dyslipidaemia, insulin resistance, systemic inflammation, a prothrombotic state, albuminuria, reduced eGFR, type 2 diabetes, and also HF and AF.<Cite n={1} /> MSD: adipose tissue is an active endocrine organ; adipokines and free fatty acids increase systemic inflammation and insulin resistance.<Cite n={9} /> Visceral adipocytes release leptin and angiotensin, and obesity-related hypertension is mediated largely by activation of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system.<Cite n={9} />

Russian Ministry of Health guideline: overweight and obesity are present in 25% of patients with AF; a direct correlation between the thickness and volume of epicardial adipose tissue and the risk of AF has been described, and possible mechanisms include its inflammatory effect on the atrial myocardium.<Cite n={5} /> Obesity is among the conditions with a particularly high risk of chronic HF; most patients with HFpEF have non-cardiac comorbidities, obesity among them.<Cite n={7} />

</Student>

## Symptoms

<Patient>

Obesity itself is an excess of fat tissue, and many of the complaints that come with it are caused by the conditions that accompany it.<Cite n={2} /> These can include raised blood pressure and headaches, shortness of breath on exertion and at rest, and snoring during sleep.<Cite n={2} /> Joint and back pain can also occur.<Cite n={10} />

The signs of heart conditions linked to obesity are described in the articles on [heart failure](../heart-failure/) and [atrial fibrillation](../atrial-fibrillation/).

</Patient>

<Student>

The Russian Ministry of Health guideline also lists increased sweating, menstrual disorders in women and reduced potency in men among the complaints caused by obesity-associated conditions.<Cite n={2} /> MSD: obstructive sleep apnoea in obesity often goes undiagnosed.<Cite n={10} />

</Student>

## How it is diagnosed

<Patient>

The doctor measures height and weight and calculates the body mass index (BMI): weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in metres.<Cite n={8} /> Waist circumference is measured too: it helps estimate how much fat there is inside the abdomen.<Cite n={[2, 10]} /> BMI is a practical measure, but it does not directly measure fat inside the abdomen, so it is interpreted with caution.<Cite n={3} /> BMI is not used to diagnose obesity in older people, athletes, people with a lot of muscle, pregnant women or people with marked swelling (oedema).<Cite n={2} />

To assess the heart and blood vessels, the doctor asks whether blood pressure has ever been raised and whether there has been a heart attack or stroke.<Cite n={2} /> Blood pressure is measured with a cuff that fits the size of the upper arm.<Cite n={2} /> Blood tests check cholesterol and triglycerides, glucose and glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c).<Cite n={2} /> Doctors also look for other common companions of obesity, such as sleep apnoea, fatty liver disease and depression.<Cite n={9} /> People with high blood pressure, coronary heart disease or heart failure have an ECG and/or echocardiography.<Cite n={2} />

</Patient>

<Student>

WHO: overweight is a BMI ≥25 kg/m², obesity a BMI ≥30 kg/m².<Cite n={8} /> Russian Ministry of Health guideline: the diagnostic criterion is a BMI ≥30 kg/m².<Cite n={2} /> NICE defines obesity classes: class 1, BMI 30–34.9 kg/m²; class 2, 35–39.9 kg/m²; class 3, 40 kg/m² or more. In people with a South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African–Caribbean background, cardiometabolic risk occurs at a lower BMI, so lower thresholds are used for them.<Cite n={3} />

Waist thresholds differ between sources. Russian Ministry of Health guideline: a waist circumference ≥94 cm in men and ≥80 cm in women is the diagnostic criterion for abdominal obesity.<Cite n={2} /> ESC 2021 (WHO thresholds): at a waist circumference >94 cm in men and >80 cm in women, further weight gain should be avoided; at >102 cm and >88 cm, weight reduction is recommended.<Cite n={1} /> NICE classifies central adiposity by waist-to-height ratio: 0.4–0.49, no increased health risk; 0.5–0.59, increased health risk; 0.6 or more, further increased health risk.<Cite n={3} />

Russian Ministry of Health guideline: up to 20% of patients with invasively proven HFpEF, especially those with obesity, have natriuretic peptide levels below the diagnostic threshold.<Cite n={7} />

</Student>

## How it is treated

<Patient>

Obesity is a chronic disease: after weight loss, weight often comes back, so it needs lifelong management and follow-up.<Cite n={[2, 9]} /> Losing weight lowers blood pressure, improves blood lipids and lowers the risk of type 2 diabetes, and so lowers the risk of heart and blood vessel disease.<Cite n={1} /> Even a modest weight loss helps if it is maintained.<Cite n={[1, 9]} />

The basis of treatment is a change in lifestyle: eating habits and more physical activity. This is the first, essential and ongoing step of treatment.<Cite n={2} /> For weight to go down, the energy taken in with food has to be lower than the energy the body uses; restrictive, nutritionally unbalanced diets do not work in the long term and can be harmful.<Cite n={3} /> For the heart, a healthy diet is recommended in the long term.<Cite n={1} /> Physical activity is an integral part of treatment,<Cite n={2} /> and it is beneficial even if weight does not go down.<Cite n={3} />

If lifestyle changes are not enough, the doctor may add medicines for obesity; they are used together with diet and physical activity.<Cite n={[2, 3]} /> After such medicines are stopped, weight may come back.<Cite n={9} /> Food supplements for weight loss are not recommended: there is no evidence that they are effective or safe.<Cite n={2} />

In severe obesity, bariatric surgery is the most effective treatment.<Cite n={9} /> It is considered for people at high risk when lifestyle changes have not helped them lose weight and keep it off.<Cite n={1} /> After such surgery, conditions such as heart and blood vessel disease, high blood pressure, sleep apnoea and type 2 diabetes can improve.<Cite n={3} />

In people with obesity and atrial fibrillation, weight loss reduces symptoms and prevents the arrhythmia from coming back.<Cite n={[1, 4]} /> High blood pressure, blood lipid disorders and diabetes are treated straight away, without waiting for weight to go down.<Cite n={3} /> The doctor decides with the patient which treatment is needed; do not start, stop or change medicines on your own.

</Patient>

<Student>

ESC 2021: weight reduction is recommended in people with overweight and obesity to lower blood pressure, dyslipidaemia and the risk of type 2 diabetes (I A); maintaining even a modest weight loss (5–10% of baseline) has favourable effects on blood pressure, lipids and glycaemic control.<Cite n={1} /> Russian Ministry of Health guideline: in people with obesity, a 5–10% reduction in body weight from baseline significantly lowers the risk of cardiovascular complications.<Cite n={6} /> ESC 2024 (AF): weight loss is recommended as part of comprehensive risk factor management in overweight and obese individuals with AF to reduce symptoms and AF burden, with a target of 10% or more reduction in body weight (I B); weight reduction should be considered in obese individuals to prevent AF (IIa B).<Cite n={4} />

Russian Ministry of Health guideline: medicines registered for the treatment of obesity are recommended when non-drug treatment does not achieve clinically significant weight loss, and/or to maintain the result.<Cite n={2} /> MSD lists GLP-1 receptor agonists among medicines that mimic or enhance naturally occurring hormones regulating appetite.<Cite n={9} /> ESC 2021: anti-obesity medicines with protective effects against ASCVD may be considered.<Cite n={1} />

ESC 2021: bariatric surgery should be considered in people with obesity at high risk when lifestyle change has not achieved and maintained weight loss (IIa B).<Cite n={1} /> Russian Ministry of Health guideline: indications are morbid obesity in patients aged 18–60 years after ineffective conservative treatment, with a BMI >40 kg/m², or a BMI >35 kg/m² with severe conditions (type 2 diabetes, joint disease, obstructive sleep apnoea) whose course can be influenced by weight loss.<Cite n={2} />

ESC 2021: an "obesity paradox" has been described in patients with HF, with lower mortality at a higher BMI, but it must be interpreted with caution (reverse causation, other biases); the effects of intentional weight loss in HF remain unclear.<Cite n={1} />

</Student>

## When to call an ambulance

Obesity itself is not an emergency: if hospital care is needed for it, it is planned in advance.<Cite n={2} /> But obesity raises the risk of coronary heart disease, stroke and heart failure, which can become emergencies.<Cite n={[9, 10]} /> For chest pain or pressure, severe shortness of breath, fainting or signs of a stroke, call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK) at once: this is the site's general safety rule. The signs of a heart attack are described in the article on [myocardial infarction](../myocardial-infarction/), and the signs of acute heart failure in the article on [heart failure](../heart-failure/).
