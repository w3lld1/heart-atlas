Heart failure is not a single disease but a clinical syndrome: because of a problem with the structure or function of the heart, it fills with blood less well or pumps it out less well.<Cite n={[2, 5]} /> It can be chronic or acute.<Cite n={5} />

## Why it happens

<Patient>

Heart failure usually does not appear on its own but as the result of other heart conditions. In Russia the main causes are high blood pressure and coronary heart disease, when the heart does not get enough blood through its own arteries.<Cite n={2} /> It can also be caused by heart valve disease, diseases of the heart muscle and heart rhythm disorders.<Cite n={5} />

</Patient>

<Student>

The Russian Ministry of Health guideline names hypertension and coronary heart disease as the main causes of chronic heart failure (CHF).<Cite n={2} /> Worldwide, ischaemic heart disease is the most common cause; hypertension causes HF even in the absence of coronary artery disease; other causes include valvular heart disease, cardiomyopathies, tachycardia and arrhythmias.<Cite n={5} />

A decrease in cardiac output activates the neuroendocrine system: the sympathetic nervous system and the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system.<Cite n={5} /> Angiotensin II increases cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and interstitial fibrosis, that is, myocardial remodelling.<Cite n={5} />

</Student>

## Symptoms

<Patient>

The most common symptom is shortness of breath.<Cite n={[2, 5]} /> It becomes hard to breathe when lying down, and a person may wake up at night short of breath.<Cite n={2} /> Activities that used to be easy become harder, and tiredness and swelling of the legs appear, especially around the ankles.<Cite n={[2, 5]} /> Weight gain can be a sign that the body is holding on to fluid.<Cite n={5} />

</Patient>

<Student>

Typical symptoms according to the Russian Ministry of Health guideline are breathlessness, orthopnoea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea, reduced exercise tolerance and ankle swelling.<Cite n={2} /> Breathlessness is the most common symptom, but it also occurs in many other conditions.<Cite n={2} /> The most specific signs are elevated jugular venous pressure, hepatojugular reflux and a third heart sound (gallop rhythm).<Cite n={2} /> Daily weighing helps to assess fluid retention.<Cite n={5} />

Symptom severity is described by functional class, from I (no limitation of physical activity) to IV (any physical activity causes discomfort).<Cite n={2} />

</Student>

## How it is diagnosed

<Patient>

The doctor asks about symptoms and examines you. Then a blood test for natriuretic peptide is ordered: if the level is normal, heart failure is usually unlikely.<Cite n={[2, 3]} /> An essential test is an ultrasound scan of the heart (echocardiography): it shows how the heart is built and how it works.<Cite n={2} /> An ECG is also done and, when needed, a chest X-ray.<Cite n={2} />

</Patient>

<Student>

A diagnosis of CHF requires typical symptoms, supporting clinical signs and evidence of left ventricular systolic and/or diastolic dysfunction; natriuretic peptides help to rule out CHF.<Cite n={2} /> When symptoms develop gradually rather than acutely, NT-proBNP below 125 pg/mL and BNP below 35 pg/mL indicate that CHF is absent.<Cite n={2} /> In atrial fibrillation, the diagnostic criteria of the Russian Ministry of Health guideline use higher values: NT-proBNP above 365 pg/mL and BNP above 105 pg/mL.<Cite n={2} /> NICE recommends measuring NT-proBNP in everyone with suspected HF.<Cite n={3} /> In suspected acute HF, a single measurement of natriuretic peptides is also used, followed by transthoracic echocardiography if they are raised.<Cite n={4} /> To rule out acute HF, NICE uses different thresholds: BNP below 100 ng/L and NT-proBNP below 300 ng/L (1 ng/L equals 1 pg/mL).<Cite n={4} />

Echocardiography is recommended for all patients with suspected HF, and a 12-lead ECG for all patients with CHF.<Cite n={2} /> A normal chest X-ray does not rule out HF when the clinical picture fits.<Cite n={5} />

The left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) groups given here follow the 2024 Russian Ministry of Health guideline: CHF with reduced (40% or less), mildly reduced (41–49%) and preserved (50% or more) ejection fraction.<Cite n={2} /> The 2026 ESC guidelines revised the classification by ejection fraction, so European and Russian groups may differ; the 2026 ESC guidelines also introduced a stage-based classification with greater emphasis on prevention.<Cite n={1} /> The American classification also uses stages: A (at risk for HF), B (pre-HF: structural changes without symptoms), C (symptomatic HF) and D (advanced HF).<Cite n={5} />

</Student>

## How it is treated

<Patient>

The doctor chooses treatment together with the patient. Its aim is to ease symptoms and improve quality of life.<Cite n={5} /> When the ejection fraction is reduced, several medicines that lower the risk of hospital admission and death are usually combined, and water tablets (diuretics) ease symptoms caused by excess fluid.<Cite n={[3, 5]} /> Some people are fitted with a device: a defibrillator that protects against sudden cardiac arrest, or a resynchronisation pacemaker.<Cite n={5} /> Physical activity through a cardiac rehabilitation programme, healthy eating and a healthy weight are important.<Cite n={[3, 5]} /> Do not start or change treatment on your own.

</Patient>

<Student>

For CHF with reduced ejection fraction, guidelines name several drug classes: ACE inhibitors or an angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitor (ARNI; sacubitril/valsartan), beta-blockers, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and SGLT2 inhibitors.<Cite n={[3, 5]} /> Diuretics are used to relieve congestive symptoms and fluid retention.<Cite n={[3, 5]} /> The choice and combination of drugs is decided by the doctor.

Devices include an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator for primary prevention of sudden cardiac death and cardiac resynchronisation therapy in CHF with reduced ejection fraction; in selected cases, mechanical circulatory support (LVAD, ECMO).<Cite n={5} /> In acute HF, NICE recommends intravenous diuretic therapy.<Cite n={4} /> People with HF are offered a personalised, exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation programme; their care is led by a specialist multidisciplinary team working with primary care.<Cite n={3} />

</Student>

## When to call an ambulance

Acute heart failure shows itself as marked difficulty breathing, breathlessness when lying flat and attacks of breathlessness at night; it can be accompanied by cardiogenic shock, a sudden failure of blood supply to the organs.<Cite n={5} /> If breathing suddenly becomes much harder, especially at rest or lying down, or attacks of breathlessness start, call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK).
