Heart failure is not a single disease but a clinical syndrome: because of a problem with the structure or function of the heart, it fills with blood less well or pumps it out less well.<Cite n={[2, 5]} /> It can be chronic or acute.<Cite n={5} />

<Patient>

## In brief

- Heart failure means the heart cannot keep up with the work the body needs from it. It does not mean the heart has stopped.<Cite n={6} />
- It usually develops gradually, over days, months or years, but it can also start suddenly, for example with a heart attack.<Cite n={6} />
- The main signs are shortness of breath, tiredness and swollen legs.<Cite n={[2, 5]} />
- A key test is an ultrasound scan of the heart. It shows the ejection fraction, and treatment depends on it.<Cite n={[2, 6]} />
- Treatment eases symptoms. When the ejection fraction is reduced, it also lowers the risk of hospital admission and death.<Cite n={[3, 5]} />

</Patient>

<Urgent>

## When to call an ambulance

Call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK) if:

- breathing suddenly becomes much harder, especially at rest or lying down;<Cite n={[5, 6]} />
- breathing becomes fast, the lips or skin turn bluish, and there is a feeling of suffocation and strong anxiety.<Cite n={6} />

These are signs of acute heart failure and of fluid on the lungs (pulmonary oedema), which are life-threatening.<Cite n={[5, 6]} /> Acute heart failure can be accompanied by cardiogenic shock, a sudden failure of blood supply to the organs.<Cite n={5} />

</Urgent>

<Patient>

## What happens to the heart

Heart failure develops when the heart contracts poorly, relaxes poorly, or both.<Cite n={6} />

- **The heart pumps blood out poorly.** The heart muscle is damaged, for example after a heart attack, and contracts weakly. Less blood leaves the heart with each beat, the pressure inside the heart rises and its chambers enlarge.<Cite n={6} />
- **The heart fills with blood poorly.** The walls of the ventricles have become thick and stiff. The ventricle cannot fill normally, so it also pumps out less blood. This happens with long-standing high blood pressure, and the heart walls also stiffen with age.<Cite n={6} />

When the heart cannot keep up, blood backs up in the veins and lungs, and the organs get less blood.<Cite n={6} /> Fluid in the lungs causes shortness of breath, fluid in the tissues causes swelling, and the muscles tire because they do not get enough blood.<Cite n={6} /> An ultrasound scan of the heart helps tell which kind a person has: it shows the thickness of the walls and the ejection fraction.<Cite n={6} />

</Patient>

## Why it happens

<Patient>

Heart failure usually does not appear on its own but as the result of other heart conditions. In Russia the main causes are high blood pressure and coronary heart disease, when the heart does not get enough blood through its own arteries.<Cite n={2} /> It can also be caused by heart valve disease, diseases of the heart muscle and heart rhythm disorders.<Cite n={5} />

Sometimes the heart suffers because of conditions of other organs. For example, in anaemia the blood carries less oxygen, so the heart has to work harder. Kidney failure and some medicines make the body hold on to fluid, and the load on the heart grows.<Cite n={6} /> Large amounts of alcohol damage the heart muscle.<Cite n={6} />

</Patient>

## Symptoms

<Patient>

Symptoms usually build up gradually.<Cite n={6} />

- **Shortness of breath.** This is the most common symptom.<Cite n={[2, 5]} /> At first it comes only with exertion, then with less and less exertion, and in the end at rest.<Cite n={6} />
- **Shortness of breath when lying down.** Breathing is harder lying down and easier sitting up. Doctors call this orthopnoea.<Cite n={[2, 6]} />
- **Attacks of breathlessness at night.** A person wakes up short of breath and feels better after sitting up. Doctors call this paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea.<Cite n={[2, 6]} />
- **Activities become harder.** What used to be easy causes breathlessness, tiredness and weakness, because the muscles do not get enough blood. Doctors call this reduced exercise tolerance.<Cite n={[2, 6]} />
- **Swelling.** Fluid collects in the feet, ankles and legs. In a person who lies down a lot, swelling appears in the lower back. When there is a lot of fluid, it also collects in the abdomen.<Cite n={[2, 6]} />
- **Weight gain.** Weight rises because the body holds on to fluid.<Cite n={[5, 6]} />
- **Nausea, bloating, poor appetite.** This happens when fluid collects in the liver and stomach.<Cite n={6} />

In older people, heart failure sometimes shows itself vaguely, as sleepiness or confusion.<Cite n={6} />

Doctors describe symptom severity by functional class: from I, when physical activity is not limited, to IV, when any physical activity causes discomfort.<Cite n={2} />

## Shortness of breath: the heart or something else

Shortness of breath is the most common symptom of heart failure, but it also occurs in many other conditions.<Cite n={2} /> So a diagnosis is not made from breathlessness alone. Only a doctor can find its cause.

During the examination the doctor looks for signs that point to heart failure more specifically:<Cite n={2} />

- swollen neck veins: the pressure in them is raised;<Cite n={[2, 6]} />
- an extra third heart sound heard with a stethoscope (gallop rhythm).<Cite n={2} />

A blood test also helps: natriuretic peptides build up in the blood in heart failure and rise less often with other causes of breathlessness.<Cite n={6} /> An ultrasound scan of the heart shows how the heart works.<Cite n={[2, 6]} />

</Patient>

## How it is diagnosed

<Patient>

The doctor asks about symptoms and examines you. Tests then answer three questions: is it heart failure, what kind is it, and what caused it.<Cite n={6} />

| Test | Why it is done |
|---|---|
| Blood test for natriuretic peptide (BNP or NT-proBNP) | To check whether this could be heart failure. If the level is normal, heart failure is usually unlikely.<Cite n={[2, 3]} /> |
| Ultrasound scan of the heart (echocardiography) | The main test, done for everyone with suspected heart failure. Shows how the heart contracts and relaxes, how thick its walls are, how the valves work, and the ejection fraction.<Cite n={[2, 6]} /> |
| ECG | To check the heart rhythm and quickly see whether there has been a heart attack.<Cite n={[2, 6]} /> |
| Chest X-ray | To see an enlarged heart and fluid in the lungs. A normal X-ray does not rule out heart failure.<Cite n={[5, 6]} /> |
| Other blood tests | To find the cause of heart failure and conditions that make it worse or complicate treatment.<Cite n={6} /> |
| Heart MRI (not for everyone) | To see inflammation and scarring in the heart muscle and the work of the right ventricle in more detail.<Cite n={6} /> |

## Ejection fraction: what the numbers mean

The ejection fraction is the share of blood that the left ventricle pushes out with each beat. It is measured during an ultrasound scan of the heart.<Cite n={6} /> A normal left ventricle pumps out about 55–60% of the blood in it.<Cite n={6} />

The 2024 Russian Ministry of Health guideline divides heart failure into three groups by ejection fraction:<Cite n={2} />

| Ejection fraction | Group | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 40% or less | reduced | The heart contracts weakly.<Cite n={6} /> |
| 41–49% | mildly reduced | An intermediate group.<Cite n={2} /> |
| 50% or more | preserved | Ejection fraction is preserved; with symptoms, the heart probably fills poorly.<Cite n={6} /> |

The 2026 European guidelines revised these groups, so European groups may differ.<Cite n={1} />

Treatment depends on the group:<Cite n={6} />

- with a reduced ejection fraction, four groups of medicines that prolong life are usually combined;<Cite n={6} />
- with a mildly reduced one, some or all of these groups are used, but their benefit here is less well proven;<Cite n={6} />
- with a preserved one, one group of medicines is usually recommended for everyone, water tablets (diuretics) for fluid retention, and the others only for some people.<Cite n={6} />

</Patient>

## How it is treated

<Patient>

The doctor chooses treatment together with the patient. Its aims are to ease symptoms, improve quality of life, lower the risk of sudden worsening and prolong life.<Cite n={[5, 6]} /> Treatment has several parts:<Cite n={6} />

- **Treating the cause.** For example, a faulty heart valve can often be corrected by surgery, and high blood pressure can be lowered with medicines.<Cite n={6} />
- **Medicines.** Which ones depends on the ejection fraction. When it is reduced, a combination of several medicines lowers the risk of hospital admission and death. Water tablets (diuretics) remove excess fluid and ease breathlessness and swelling.<Cite n={[3, 5, 6]} />
- **Devices.** Some people are fitted with a defibrillator that protects against sudden cardiac arrest, or a resynchronisation pacemaker.<Cite n={[5, 6]} />
- **Physical activity.** It is planned through a cardiac rehabilitation programme.<Cite n={[3, 5]} />

Take medicines regularly and do not let them run out: missed medicines are a common cause of worsening.<Cite n={6} /> Do not start or change treatment on your own.

## Living with heart failure

Much of heart failure care happens at home, so it is important to know the early signs of worsening.<Cite n={6} />

- **Weigh yourself every day.** Best in the morning, after using the toilet and before breakfast, on the same scales and in similar clothes. Write the result down.<Cite n={6} />
- **Watch for weight gain.** A gain of more than 1 kg in a day is an early sign of fluid retention. Tell your doctor about it.<Cite n={6} />
- **Watch your breathing.** If breathlessness comes with less exertion than before, tell your doctor.<Cite n={6} />
- **Do not take new medicines without your doctor**, even ones sold without a prescription. Some of them, such as some medicines for joint pain, make the body hold on to fluid.<Cite n={6} />
- **Stay active.** Stay as physically active as your condition allows. Your doctor plans the amount of exercise.<Cite n={[3, 6]} />
- **Do not smoke and limit alcohol.** Smoking damages blood vessels, and large amounts of alcohol damage the heart muscle.<Cite n={6} />
- **Keep a healthy weight.** Extra weight makes the heart work harder.<Cite n={[5, 6]} />
- **Ask your doctor about a flu vaccine.** Flu can suddenly make heart failure worse.<Cite n={6} />

## Questions for your doctor

You can take these questions to your appointment:

- What is my ejection fraction, and which group of heart failure do I have?
- What caused it in my case?
- What is each of my medicines for?
- How much physical activity is safe for me?
- At what weight gain or which symptoms should I call you, and when should I call an ambulance?

</Patient>
