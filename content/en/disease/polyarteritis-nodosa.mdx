Polyarteritis nodosa is a rare systemic inflammation of the artery wall (vasculitis) that mainly affects medium-sized arteries and sometimes small ones.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> It most often develops in middle age, usually in people in their 50s, but it can occur at any age.<Cite n={2} /> Some Russian documents call the disease periarteritis nodosa.<Cite n={[4, 5]} />

The Russian Ministry of Health has no separate guideline on this disease; it is mentioned in the guidelines on other diseases, for example on chronic hepatitis B, on arterial hypertension in children and on myocarditis.<Cite n={[3, 4, 5]} /> Other types of vasculitis are covered in the articles on [giant cell arteritis](../giant-cell-arteritis/), [Takayasu arteritis](../takayasu-arteritis/) and [Kawasaki disease](../kawasaki-disease/).

## Why it happens

<Patient>

The cause is usually unknown; the immune system is thought to be involved.<Cite n={1} /> Sometimes the disease is triggered by viral hepatitis B, rarely by hepatitis C, or by medicines.<Cite n={2} /> According to MSD, about 20% of patients have hepatitis B or C.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> The Russian Ministry of Health guideline on chronic hepatitis B names polyarteritis nodosa among the manifestations of this infection outside the liver; sometimes hepatitis B first shows itself through them, even when blood chemistry tests show no liver damage.<Cite n={3} />

The disease affects arteries; it does not affect veins.<Cite n={1} />

</Patient>

<Student>

Polyarteritis nodosa (PAN) is a systemic necrotising vasculitis of medium-sized and occasionally small muscular arteries, causing secondary tissue ischaemia.<Cite n={[1, 6]} /> Postcapillary venules and veins are not involved, there are no granulomas or giant cells, glomerulonephritis is not a feature, and the lungs are usually spared.<Cite n={[1, 6]} />

</Student>

## Symptoms

<Patient>

The disease often begins with general symptoms: fever, tiredness, night sweats, loss of appetite and weight, weakness, and aching muscles and joints.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> The other symptoms depend on which organ is short of blood; sometimes only one organ is affected.<Cite n={[1, 2]} />

- Nerves: patchy numbness, tingling and weakness, foot drop or wrist drop.<Cite n={[1, 2]} />
- Skin: a net-like pattern (livedo), tender lumps, ulcers, and sometimes death of tissue in the fingers or toes.<Cite n={1} />
- Kidneys: high [blood pressure](../hypertension/), which may rise quickly, blood in the urine, kidney failure.<Cite n={[1, 2]} />
- Bowel: severe abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, bloody diarrhoea, a hole in the bowel (perforation).<Cite n={[1, 2]} />
- Heart: it is affected less often; in some patients the coronary arteries are involved; this usually causes no symptoms but can cause [angina](../coronary-heart-disease/); [heart attack](../myocardial-infarction/) and [heart failure](../heart-failure/) are possible.<Cite n={[1, 2]} />
- Brain: headache, seizures, sometimes a stroke.<Cite n={[1, 2]} />
- Testes: pain and inflammation (orchitis).<Cite n={[1, 2]} />

The disease can get worse quickly and be fatal within several months, or it can develop gradually.<Cite n={2} />

</Patient>

<Student>

The Russian Ministry of Health guideline on arterial hypertension in children lists periarteritis nodosa among the causes of renovascular hypertension (see [renal artery stenosis](../renal-artery-stenosis/)).<Cite n={4} /> Mesenteric vasculitis can cause malabsorption and intestinal perforation (see [mesenteric ischaemia](../mesenteric-ischemia/)).<Cite n={1} /> The Russian Ministry of Health guideline on [myocarditis](../myocarditis/) lists periarteritis nodosa among the systemic diseases in which myocarditis occurs.<Cite n={5} />

</Student>

## How it is diagnosed

<Patient>

The diagnosis can be difficult to make.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> A doctor may suspect the disease when several of these occur together: unexplained fever, joint pain, lumps and ulcers on the skin, pain in the abdomen or limbs, a new foot drop or wrist drop, rapidly rising blood pressure.<Cite n={1} />

Blood tests are not specific, but testing for hepatitis B and C should always be done.<Cite n={1} /> The Russian Ministry of Health guideline on chronic hepatitis B suggests considering a blood test for the virus antigen (HBsAg) in polyarteritis nodosa.<Cite n={3} />

The diagnosis is confirmed by a biopsy (a small piece of an affected organ is examined under a microscope) or by angiography, an X-ray of the blood vessels with contrast dye.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> The biopsy is taken where there are signs of the disease: a biopsy of tissue that looks healthy is often useless.<Cite n={1} /> Electromyography and nerve conduction studies confirm nerve involvement and sometimes help choose the biopsy site.<Cite n={2} />

</Patient>

<Student>

Angiography shows saccular or fusiform aneurysms and stenoses of the mesenteric, hepatic and renal arteries and their branches.<Cite n={6} /> A superficial punch biopsy of the skin misses the lesions, so a deep biopsy is needed; in neuropathy ACR/VF conditionally recommend a combined nerve and muscle biopsy.<Cite n={[1, 6]} />

</Student>

## How it is treated

<Patient>

Treatment depends on how severe the disease is.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> Its goal is a period without signs of the disease (remission).<Cite n={2} /> Medicines that may have triggered the disease are stopped.<Cite n={2} /> Treatment that suppresses the immune system (immunosuppressive treatment) is used; the doctor decides what it will be.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> This treatment weakens the body's defence against infections.<Cite n={2} />

High blood pressure is treated vigorously: this protects the internal organs.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> If the disease is linked to hepatitis B, treatment to suppress inflammation is combined with antiviral treatment.<Cite n={[1, 2]} />

Treatment aims to prevent new damage but often cannot reverse damage that has already occurred.<Cite n={2} /> A weakened artery may rupture, and a heart attack can happen long after the inflammation has been treated.<Cite n={2} />

</Patient>

<Student>

ACR/VF classify disease with life- or organ-threatening manifestations as severe: renal disease, mononeuritis multiplex, muscle disease, mesenteric ischaemia, coronary involvement, and limb or digit ischaemia.<Cite n={6} /> ACR/VF do not cover hepatitis B-associated PAN or cutaneous PAN, as these are managed differently.<Cite n={6} /> In nerve or muscle involvement ACR/VF conditionally recommend physical therapy.<Cite n={6} />

According to the MSD Consumer Version, fewer than 15% of patients survive 5 years without treatment and more than 80% with treatment.<Cite n={2} /> The MSD Professional Version gives 5-year survival with treatment as about 80%; it may be lower in patients with hepatitis B.<Cite n={1} /> ACR/VF give a 5-year mortality of 40% for severe PAN.<Cite n={6} /> The prognosis is worse with renal, gastrointestinal or neurological involvement.<Cite n={[1, 2]} /> Relapses are less common than in other vasculitides; according to ACR/VF, most patients have monophasic disease.<Cite n={[1, 6]} />

</Student>

## When to call an ambulance

Complications include perforation of the bowel or gallbladder (an "acute abdomen"), rupture of an artery with internal bleeding, heart attack, stroke and bleeding into the brain.<Cite n={[1, 2]} />

Call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK) for sudden severe abdominal pain and for severe pain in the lower back with blood in the urine.<Cite n={1} /> If there are signs of a stroke (sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body, difficulty speaking, a drooping face), call your local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK) straight away. The signs of a heart attack are described in the article on [myocardial infarction](../myocardial-infarction/). As with any heart condition, call an ambulance for fainting, chest pain or severe shortness of breath: this is the site's general safety rule, repeated at the bottom of every page.

New weakness of a foot or hand, rapidly rising blood pressure, and fever and weight loss without an obvious cause are reasons to see a doctor without delay.<Cite n={1} />
