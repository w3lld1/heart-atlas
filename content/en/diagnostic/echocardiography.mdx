Echocardiography (echo, heart ultrasound) is a non-invasive test that uses ultrasound to show the structure of the heart and how it works.<Cite n={4} /> The standard test through the chest wall (transthoracic echocardiography) is widely available, low-cost and the first-line test for many heart conditions.<Cite n={4} />

## What it shows

<Patient>

The screen shows the walls and dividing walls (septa) of each heart chamber, so the doctor can judge the size of the chambers and how well they pump.<Cite n={5} /> The movement of the valve leaflets can be seen too.<Cite n={5} /> Colour Doppler shows blood flow: its direction, its speed and whether it is turbulent.<Cite n={5} /> This is how a narrowed or leaking valve is found, for example in [aortic stenosis](../../disease/aortic-stenosis/).<Cite n={4} />

When [heart failure](../../disease/heart-failure/) is suspected, echo shows how well the left ventricle contracts and relaxes, whether there is serious valve disease, and whether blood is leaking between the heart chambers (a shunt), as in an [atrial septal defect](../../disease/atrial-septal-defect/).<Cite n={[1, 4]} /> The scan also shows fluid around the heart (pericardial effusion).<Cite n={[4, 5]} />

How the chambers and valves are built is explained in the articles ["Atria and ventricles"](../../anatomy/heart-chambers/) and ["Heart valves"](../../anatomy/heart-valves/).

</Patient>

<Student>

The standard transthoracic imaging windows are parasternal, apical, subcostal and suprasternal notch.<Cite n={5} /> Each view is best for particular questions: the apical four-chamber view for wall motion abnormalities of the inferior septal and anterolateral walls of the left ventricle and for mitral and tricuspid valve disease; the subcostal view for atrial septal defect, pericardial effusion and cardiac tamponade; the suprasternal view for diseases of the aorta such as aortic dissection and coarctation.<Cite n={4} />

Imaging modes:

- two-dimensional (B-mode) imaging displays the image in shades of grey;<Cite n={5} />
- M-mode has superior time resolution, which improves the display of rapidly moving structures;<Cite n={5} />
- colour Doppler imaging displays timing within the cardiac cycle, relative velocity, direction of flow and the presence of turbulence;<Cite n={5} />
- pulsed-wave Doppler measures blood flow velocity at a particular depth.<Cite n={5} />

An ECG is recorded during the study: a good-quality ECG signal is needed to time measurements to the phases of the cardiac cycle.<Cite n={5} /> End-diastole is the first frame immediately after mitral leaflet closure; the LV end-systolic dimension is taken at the smallest cavity dimension.<Cite n={5} /> More about the phases in the article ["Cardiac cycle"](../../anatomy/cardiac-cycle/).

</Student>

## When it is done

<Patient>

Clinical guidelines list echo among the main tests in several situations:

- suspected heart failure: under NICE guidance an [NT-proBNP blood test](../natriuretic-peptides/) is done first, and if the level is above the threshold in the guideline, the person is referred for echo;<Cite n={1} />
- a heart murmur, even without other symptoms, if the doctor suspects valve disease from the type of murmur, family history, the person's age or other conditions;<Cite n={2} />
- suspected chronic coronary syndrome ([coronary heart disease](../../disease/coronary-heart-disease/)): echo gives important information about how the heart works and how it is built, and helps find other causes of chest pain.<Cite n={3} />

Symptoms can also be a reason for the test: chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting or palpitations.<Cite n={4} />

</Patient>

<Student>

NICE NG106 recommends measuring NT-proBNP in people with suspected heart failure. Adults with suspected heart failure and an NT-proBNP level above 2,000 ng/litre (236 pmol/litre) should be referred urgently for specialist assessment and transthoracic echocardiography within 2 weeks; with a level between 400 and 2,000 ng/litre (47 to 236 pmol/litre), within 6 weeks.<Cite n={1} /> These are thresholds for the urgency of referral, not reference ranges for the test: reference values depend on the laboratory; see the article ["BNP and NT-proBNP test"](../natriuretic-peptides/). According to NG106, echo is performed to exclude important valve disease, assess the systolic (and diastolic) function of the left ventricle and detect intracardiac shunts.<Cite n={1} />

NICE NG208 recommends considering an echocardiogram for adults with a murmur and no other signs or symptoms if valve disease is suspected based on the nature of the murmur, family history, age (especially if over 75) or medical history, for example a history of atrial fibrillation.<Cite n={2} />

According to the 2024 ESC Guidelines on chronic coronary syndromes, decreased LV function and/or regional wall motion abnormalities may increase the suspicion of ischaemic myocardial damage; echo also helps detect alternative causes of chest pain and valvular heart disease.<Cite n={3} />

</Student>

## How it is done

<Patient>

Your doctor or the clinic doing the test will tell you how to prepare, especially for a transoesophageal echo: follow their instructions.

For a standard (transthoracic) echo you lie on your left side.<Cite n={[4, 5]} /> ECG electrodes are placed on your chest, and the probe is held against your chest, including to the left of the breastbone.<Cite n={5} /> For some images you lie on your back with your stomach muscles relaxed, and you may be asked to hold your breath after breathing out.<Cite n={5} /> Complications of transthoracic and stress echo are infrequent.<Cite n={4} />

**Transoesophageal echo.** The probe is passed through the mouth into the oesophagus (food pipe), so this is an invasive test.<Cite n={4} /> It is done when a standard scan does not give an answer, for example when infective endocarditis is suspected.<Cite n={4} /> The throat is numbed, a mouth guard is used and medicines for sedation are given; the test has its own risks, including injury to the oesophagus.<Cite n={4} />

**Stress echocardiography.** Images are taken during exercise on a treadmill or exercise bike, or after a medicine is given in place of exercise.<Cite n={[3, 4]} /> This looks for areas of heart muscle that are not getting enough blood.<Cite n={3} />

</Patient>

<Student>

TOE is indicated when transthoracic echo is non-diagnostic due to patient characteristics, to diagnose infective endocarditis in patients with moderate to high pre-test probability, and to guide percutaneous non-coronary cardiac interventions.<Cite n={4} /> Absolute contraindications include oesophageal disease (stricture, perforation, tumour, diverticulum) and a history of upper gastrointestinal bleeding; reported complications include bleeding, oesophageal perforation, pharyngeal haematoma, aspiration, laryngospasm, arrhythmia and toxic methaemoglobinaemia.<Cite n={4} />

Stress echocardiography detects myocardial ischaemia by assessing regional systolic wall-thickening abnormalities.<Cite n={3} /> Stress modalities: exercise (treadmill or bicycle), intravenous dobutamine, or vasodilators.<Cite n={3} /> StatPearls lists intermediate and high pre-test probability of coronary artery disease among the indications.<Cite n={4} />

</Student>

## What the results mean

<Patient>

The echo report describes the size of the chambers and how they work, and the state of the valves.<Cite n={5} /> The doctor interprets the report together with your symptoms, examination, [ECG](../ecg/) and other tests. Echo not only helps diagnose heart disease but also gives information that guides treatment.<Cite n={4} />

</Patient>

<Student>

The ASE recommends calculating LV volumes and ejection fraction from 2D images using the biplane summation-of-discs method; calculation from linear measurements is not recommended.<Cite n={5} />

</Student>

## Limitations

<Patient>

In some people the heart is hard to see through the chest wall. Then an ultrasound contrast agent or other tests may be used: cardiac MRI, transoesophageal echo or radionuclide angiography.<Cite n={[1, 3]} />

</Patient>

<Student>

Ultrasound contrast agents may be required in people with obesity and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and in stress echo must be used if it is evident at baseline that not all segments may be visible.<Cite n={3} /> When endocardial definition is reduced, the ASE highly recommends ultrasound enhancing agents for quantifying LV volumes.<Cite n={5} /> In suspected coronary artery disease, cardiac MRI is an alternative when echo (including with ultrasound contrast) is inconclusive.<Cite n={3} />

</Student>
